# Start here

We put this repository together for your interview. Take a look before we talk. There's nothing
to hand in and no setup you have to get working.

## What it is

Permit Desk is the service behind a building permit portal for the City of Mountport. Mountport
isn't a real place. Somebody applies for a building permit, and this is the thing that carries
the application through zoning, plan review, a round of corrections, fees, payment, and issuing
the permit at the end. Four other city systems make the real decisions. Permit Desk coordinates
them and keeps the record.

It's a whole working system rather than a snippet, and it's written the way a real team on a
deadline writes things. We haven't cleaned it up or annotated it.

## What we'd like you to do

Read it. An hour or two is plenty, and skimming is fine.

Running it is optional, but it's worth it. The README says how and it comes up with one
command. A few things about this system only make sense once you've watched an application
move through it.

Then show up with opinions. The kind of thing we want to hear:

- what you noticed
- what you'd fix first, and why that and not something else
- what you'd want to know before this was open to the public
- what you'd leave alone

That last one matters. Knowing what not to touch is a big part of the job.

## Using AI

Point an AI at it if that's how you work. Most people will, and we'd rather see how you
actually work than watch you pretend.

We'll ask about it during the session. That's not a trap and it won't count against you.
What you reached for, what you kept and what you threw out is worth talking about on its
own, and using these tools well is part of the job now.

It'll give you a list, and a ranked one with a plan attached if you ask for that. What it
can't do is sit in the room with us. We'll push back on whatever you bring, and what we're
interested in is why you'd order it that way, what it costs you if you're wrong, and where
you'd change your mind. Come with a position you can defend rather than one you're holding.

## The interview

An hour, two of us. A bit over half of it is this codebase.

We'll start by asking what you'd stop a launch for. Then we'll open a few places in the code
and ask what you make of them. After that you steer, go wherever you find interesting. We'll
finish on what the first week on the team actually looks like.

You won't be asked to write code, and there's no take-home.

## Worth saying up front

We're not counting how many problems you find. Six things with a clear argument about which one
matters most beats twenty in a list.

"I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" is a good answer. So is "I'd go ask whoever owns
that service." That's the job most days.

There's nothing you have to spot to do well. It's a codebase, we're going to talk about it, and
mostly we want to see how you think.

## If something's unclear

Ask us before we talk. It won't count against you.
